## Do current TypeSafe docs still describe Choice, Noul, independent questions, confidence semantics, and jev-1.13.0 as the PRD states?

### Takeaway
Yes. On 2026-09-23 the live TypeSafe pages still document `POST /v1/systemone`, Choice, Noul, independent parallel questions, Choice/Score `confidence` derived from the option distribution, Noul as a 0–1 probability with no separate confidence field, and `jev-1.13.0` as the version behind `jev-latest`. The jaggedness page still lists literal reading, indirection, distracting context, and no guaranteed agreement between separate questions.

### Cited Findings
- The evaluation endpoint is `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone` with `Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>` and `Content-Type: application/json`. The body is `state`, `model`, and a `questions` map. Each question key “is not sent to the underlying model and is not used in inference.” A 401 means the Authorization header is missing or invalid. Response examples use `"model": "jev-1.13.0"`. — [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api)
- Choice returns `choice`, `probabilities`, and `confidence`. Noul returns `noul` from 0 to 1. Score is a third primitive and returns `score`, `legend`, `probabilities`, and `confidence`. All three can be mixed in one call. Every question sees the same state, is evaluated independently in parallel, and one answer is not context for another. If a later question needs an earlier answer in order to build state or options, the docs say to make a second request. — [Primitives](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives); [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api)
- Choice and Score `confidence` is a 0–1 statistic computed from `probabilities`. Noul answers do not carry `confidence`. A Noul of 0.5 means yes and no are equally probable; it is not a medium score on a scale. The three-option demo approximates confidence as `(3 × largest probability − 1) / 2`. An API example shows Choice probabilities `{billing: 0.88, technical: 0.12, sales: 0.0}` with `confidence: 0.81`, so the documented confidence value is not identical to the selected option’s probability. Thresholds “depend on your domain and the performance of the model for your use case.” — [Confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence); [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api)
- The models page lists Jev 1.13 as `jev-1.13.0`. Price is $42 per billion / $0.042 per million input tokens; output tokens are free. Limits stated: 250,000 tokens per second, 1,200 requests per minute, 64k tokens per request, and 32k tokens for `state` plus the longest question. Input is text only. `jev-latest` and `jev-preview` both point to `jev-1.13.0`. The page says to pin a versioned ID when confidence thresholds were tuned against that version. Rate limits can change without notice. Jev is not fine-tuned per customer; the same weights serve every account. English is the primary training language. Customer requests are not used for training. — [Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models)
- The jaggedness page applies to `jev-1.13` and still says “Last reviewed 2026-09-17.” Named failure modes include literal reading; unreliable counting and arithmetic; unreliable date and time comparison; extra indirection; accuracy loss when state contains irrelevant detail; adversarial text; contradictory instructions and criteria; no guarantee that a Noul and a yes/no Choice, or a Noul and its negation, agree; and no text generation. Worked numbers: one ticket has Noul 0.22 beside a Choice of yes 0.01 / no 0.99 / confidence 0.97; another has refund Noul 0.72 and not-refund Noul 0.47, summing to 1.19. The page says not to carry a threshold tuned on a Noul over to a Choice. — [Jev 1.13 jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13)
- The PRD states the same request shape, Choice/Noul split, independent questions, two calls when a later question needs the selected action, Choice confidence versus Noul’s lack of a confidence field, pin `jev-1.13.0`, and the literal-reading / indirection / distracting-context / structural-consistency limits. It also says the probe’s roughly `1 + participants × (2 + candidate tasks)` layout is evidence about that prototype, not the required production layout. — [PRD §5.1–5.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- The local probe’s default model is `jev-1.13.0`, the endpoint constant is `https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone`, and the thresholds are Choice confidence 0.80, Noul yes 0.85, Noul no 0.15. Question construction is one input-kind Choice, then per participant a recipient Choice, a timing Choice, and one dependency Noul per candidate task. — [probe_jev.py](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/scripts/probe_jev.py)

### Inferences
- The PRD’s description of hosted Jev is still aligned with the official docs: a pinned `jev-1.13.0` call can classify a finite action list with Choice and score candidate yes/no edges with Noul. It cannot emit task text, invent task IDs, or execute a handler. Those limits follow from the generation failure mode and from the answer shapes.
- Nothing on the official pages reviewed on 2026-09-23 shows a model newer than `jev-1.13.0`. Pinning `jev-1.13.0` rather than `jev-latest` is still what the models page recommends once thresholds exist.
- A third primitive, Score, exists and is unused by the PRD and the probe. That is extra API surface, not a contradiction.

### Gaps
- These pages were not byte-compared with a 2026-09-21 snapshot. Silent edits after that date are possible. The only explicit review date found on the limitation page is still 2026-09-17.
- `GET /v1/models` was not called. The models page’s statement that aliases currently resolve to `jev-1.13.0` was not re-checked against a live response.
- The confidence page still does not publish a closed-form production formula outside the demo’s three-option approximation.

## Does the documented env var TYPESAFE_API_KEY vs local JEV_API_KEY matter for HTTP?

### Takeaway
No for the HTTP protocol. TypeSafe’s documented client variable is `TYPESAFE_API_KEY`. The evaluation API only requires `Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>`. The local name `JEV_API_KEY` is a process-environment convention and is not part of the request.

### Cited Findings
- The Python SDK quickstart says to set `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` and then construct `TypeSafeClient()` with no key argument. — [Python SDK](https://docs.typesafe.ai/sdk/python)
- The models page’s curl example is `Authorization: Bearer $TYPESAFE_API_KEY`. — [Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models)
- The HTTP reference specifies the header `Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>`. `401 Unauthorized` means the header is missing or invalid. The reference does not name an environment variable. — [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api)
- The probe reads `JEV_API_KEY` from the process environment or a local `.env` line and sends it only as `"Authorization": "Bearer " + key`. The PRD says TypeSafe examples use a different variable name and that the HTTP protocol does not require renaming the local variable. — [probe_jev.py](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/scripts/probe_jev.py); [PRD §5.2](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)

### Inferences
- A room service can keep `JEV_API_KEY` and still speak the documented HTTP protocol, as the probe already does. Using the official SDK’s default constructor would require `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` or an explicit key argument. Those are client-library choices, not a change to what the API accepts.
- Renaming the local variable is unnecessary for protocol compatibility. It would matter only if the project switched to a client that reads `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` automatically.

### Gaps
- Official docs pages other than the Python SDK quickstart and the models-page curl example were not exhaustively searched for every SDK language’s variable name.

## Is SemIf a local scorer without TypeSafe weights, needing a custom adapter?

### Takeaway
Yes. SemIf (formerly OpenJev) is an independent local option scorer. It does not claim TypeSafe weights, does not expose `POST /v1/systemone`, and has no Noul or Score types in its documented row. A T3 Rooms adapter would have to translate catalog questions into SemIf rows and evaluate its own thresholds. This repository has not installed or scored SemIf.

### Cited Findings
- The README title is “SemIf (formerly OpenJev).” It says the project is independent, not affiliated with or endorsed by TypeSafe, and that it “does not reproduce Jev's undisclosed model or training.” Model weights are not in the repository. Project code is MIT-licensed. — [SemIf README](https://github.com/TheoLeeCJ/SemIf/blob/master/README.md)
- The documented input row is `{id, state, question, options: [{id, description}, ...]}`. Returned probabilities “are conditional on the supplied options.” The README says to calibrate and validate them on the workload where they will make decisions. The quick start is the `semif-score` command with `--mode direct`, `--model Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B`, and a pinned revision, reading JSONL. Shared mode prefills one exact state and scores many criteria. There is no documented TypeSafe HTTP route in that README. — [SemIf README](https://github.com/TheoLeeCJ/SemIf/blob/master/README.md)
- README “Latest changes,” dated 2026-09-22 (after the 2026-09-21 local Jev experiment): PyTorch/MPS scoring for Apple Silicon; a Qwen3.8-27B EXL3 bridge; per-workload temperature calibration. “Calibration does not change the selected option.” Apple Silicon instructions say native MLX is for macOS arm64 via `pip install -e '.[test,mlx]'` and `--backend mlx`, with PyTorch/MPS as another path. — [SemIf README](https://github.com/TheoLeeCJ/SemIf/blob/master/README.md)
- On SemIf’s own table, Qwen3.5-4B native BF16 modal agreement with published Jev on a 102-row public subset is 0.845, against published Jev’s 0.883 on that subset. The README says the comparison was not a live Jev run and is not TypeSafe’s 711-row aggregate. Authored-decision balanced accuracy for the 4B direct-logit baseline is 0.813 on 144 rows. Those figures are SemIf’s workloads, not T3 Rooms instructions. — [SemIf README](https://github.com/TheoLeeCJ/SemIf/blob/master/README.md)
- The PRD already says SemIf needs a room adapter, is not a drop-in for the Jev HTTP endpoint, that local scores must not be forced into Jev’s confidence representation, and that no SemIf installation or local inference benchmark was done in that review. Hosted Jev and local SemIf are Phase 2. — [PRD §5.0](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §10](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- `IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md` reports SemIf is Python-only with pinned heavy dependencies, has no HTTP server, no Noul or Score primitive, and that a TypeScript service would need a Python sidecar. That assessment is a 2026-09-23 local review of the same repository; this pass did not re-open `pyproject.toml`. — [IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/research/IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md)

### Inferences
- A custom adapter is required. SemIf’s row is one question plus an option list. Jev’s request is a map of typed `choice`, `score`, and `noul` questions, and Choice confidence is a separate field that Noul does not have. Mapping a dependency onto a two-option or logit score is application code. The probe’s 0.80 / 0.85 / 0.15 gates are Jev-probe settings and are not SemIf outputs.
- SemIf’s published agreement with Jev on 102 public rows is evidence about SemIf’s benchmark, not evidence that SemIf would route T3 room commands. The PRD’s requirement to test the room’s own action and reference set before enabling local suggestions is still unmet in this repository.
- MPS/MLX on Apple Silicon is a local runtime path, not a Jev-compatible server. The durable queue is specified to continue with neither model running.

### Gaps
- This pass did not inventory every SemIf file for an undocumented HTTP server. The README does not describe a `systemone` drop-in.
- SemIf was not installed or scored on the room fixtures, per the assignment constraint.
- `mlx_backend.py` and the browser-demo README were not re-fetched in this pass; claims about those files remain those of the PRD and the earlier feasibility note.

- The same assessment says T3 `thread.settled` is not a task-success receipt either. `IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md` did not re-verify Herdr and records that it is “not on the implementation path.” — [HERDR_ASSESSMENT.md](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/research/HERDR_ASSESSMENT.md); [IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/research/IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md)

### Inferences
- Rejecting Herdr as the execution backend is consistent with the written gaps. A Synapse idle gate would fail Sequential review and Fan-in whenever the reviewer is idle, because eligibility is not a revisioned `after_all` edge. An `agent.wait` return would fail Run correlation, because the live docs still say it can complete on the previous turn. Building Phase 1 on Herdr would still mean writing the SQLite graph, cycle checks, revision rules, and outbox. Herdr does not shrink that scope.
- The user’s trial and the source gaps are stacked reasons, not a single measured demonstration. The trial is undiagnosed discovery and dislike. The graph and turn gaps are from documentation plus the prior source reading. Both are internally consistent with “do not block T3 Rooms on another Herdr pilot.”
- It is not consistent to treat “Herdr has no turn correlation” as proof that the T3 path is already safe. The assessment and PRD §6.4 say T3’s settled event has the same class of bug. The consistent reading is that idle, settled, and uncorrelated wait are inadequate on both backends, so the companion must correlate the turn it submitted.
- T3 Code’s absence from Herdr’s supported-agents table is consistent with a trial that “could not find the agent harnesses,” but it does not diagnose that trial. Detection failure remains an unmeasured user report.

### Gaps
- Synapse daemon/gate Python was not re-opened in this pass (no install; no clone). “No prerequisite fields in source” remains the 2026-09-22 assessment’s inspection, corroborated by the current public README’s delivery checklist, not by a fresh source grep.
- The detection failure’s cause is explicitly unknown.
- No side-by-side run shows T3 correlating turns better than Herdr.

## After T3 adapter + SQLite queue exist, what remaining product-shape failures can still fail section 11?

### Takeaway
Several section 11 rows can still fail as ordinary product-code bugs after a working T3 adapter and SQLite outbox exist. The ones that can sink the plan without any missing T3 API are: treating a successful turn that contains a prose blocker as completion (releasing dependents); binding dependents to a superseded prerequisite revision; treating cancel as if it were fail, or failing to block dependents on cancel; and treating agent/steering text as a successful assignment. An undefined context budget can also fail the Context row even when full-text reads work.

### Cited Findings

**Prose-success release (section 11 Sequential review, Failures, Run correlation)**

- Completion is “the adapter observes that correlated turn's successful terminal state, no outstanding blocking request, and the final response/artifact metadata needed for the handoff has been ingested.” “A provider may return a normal final response containing a prose blocker without an equivalent native blocked state.” The first release “must expose that limitation and let the user mark a task blocked; it must not advertise universal semantic completion detection.” A later `report_task_outcome` facility is Phase 2. — [PRD §6.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §10](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Section 4.4’s catalog has no `task.mark_blocked` / `report_task_outcome` row. `task.interrupt` and `task.retry` exist; user mark-blocked does not. An instruction already accepted by T3 “requires an explicit interrupt/follow-up operation; changing the local card cannot retract a delivered prompt.” — [PRD §4.3](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §4.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Sequential review requires the review to receive completed output “exactly once.” Failures requires failed/interrupted/input-blocked predecessors to keep dependents blocked with the reason visible. If the predecessor’s turn is natively successful and the prose says “blocked on X,” the written release rule still fires. — [PRD §11](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)

**Revision graphs (section 11 Editing, Sequential review, Fan-in)**

- Dependencies “bind to specific task revisions, not an alias's next transition to idle.” Editing a pending instruction “creates a new revision/command identity.” `task.update` is allowed before dispatch and “validate[s] the complete replacement and create[s] a new revision.” — [PRD §6.3](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.5](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §4.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- The PRD never says whether dependents of revision 1 follow revision 2, stay on the superseded revision, or become blocked. Section 11 has no acceptance row titled “edit prerequisite revision.” The Editing row only requires that pending tasks can be changed/cancelled and that already accepted tasks are not falsely shown as retracted. — [PRD §4.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §11](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- `task.retry` “record[s] a new attempt” and takes “explicit dependent-task handling.” Section 6.5’s “identical retry” reuses the command id and the frozen briefing. The word “retry” covers both cases. Section 6.3 forbids treating unrelated later work as the prerequisite’s completion. — [PRD §4.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.3](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.5](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)

**Cancel vs fail (section 11 Failures, Contract validation)**

- `task.cancel` applies to work T3 has not accepted and must “expose the resulting block on dependent tasks.” The state diagram’s `queued → blocked` trigger is “prerequisite fails or binding unavailable,” not cancellation. Cancel edges are drawn only from `queued` and `held`. — [PRD §4.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.2](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Section 11 Failures: failed, interrupted, and input-blocked predecessors “keep dependents blocked.” Section 6.3 says unresolved native input does not release dependents; it does not say it moves them to `blocked`. `needs_input` is a state on the predecessor, not a listed blocked trigger. — [PRD §11](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.2](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.3](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)

**Steering / false-success (section 11 Run correlation, Sequential review, Manual coverage)**

- “Only a submitted user message can create assignments. Assistant outputs, quoted examples, and tool logs are context, not automatic dispatch triggers.” “Agent replies never recursively create work merely because they contain a mention.” Another participant’s suggestions remain that participant’s statements, not new instructions from the user. — [PRD §5.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §7](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Direct T3 activity “can occupy a session but must not accidentally satisfy an unrelated room prerequisite.” Dispatch acknowledgement, a commentary message, or an idle thread “observed without run correlation is insufficient.” Do not use `thread.settled` alone. — [PRD §9](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- `IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md` observed that T3 emits no `turn.completed` event and that `thread.settled` is used for both settle and auto-settle; it recommends keying on the submitted `turnId` plus `turn-diff-completed` and `latestTurn.state == completed`. That is adapter correlation. It does not decide what to do when that native success contains a blocker, a mention of `@sol2`, or an unrelated later turn. — [IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/research/IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md)

**Context budget (section 11 Context)**

- “Preserve every unseen visible message while it fits the delivery budget.” Over budget, condensation is shown, source references are kept, and originals stay accessible. “Jev cannot write these summaries.” A generative summarizer is optional later. User-pinned decisions are mentioned as an initial tactic and are not a catalog action. The budget size, the condensation algorithm, and the pin action are not defined. — [PRD §7](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Context acceptance: earlier unseen decisions survive multiple sibling replies; long-message truncation is surfaced; originals remain retrievable. “Do not keep only the latest reply per sibling.” — [PRD §11](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §7](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)

**Other product-code holes that remain after an adapter exists**

- `dispatching` has one success edge to `running`. Restart, rejection, and “reconcile before another dispatch” are prose in section 6.5 with no transition. The Restart acceptance test is specifically “after submission but before acknowledgement.” — [PRD §6.2](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.5](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §11](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Command-id scope is unspecified for one `task.create` that fans out to two participants. — [PRD §4.5](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.5](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Rebinding “must account for outstanding tasks” and “cannot silently send a pending task to a different session.” The blocked reason “binding unavailable” exists. The account-for algorithm does not. — [PRD §4.2](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §6.2](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)
- Participant/native-request controls are required by section 11 and have no command schema in section 4.5. — [PRD §4.4](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md); [PRD §11](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/PRD.md)

### Inferences
- These are product-code problems. A correct adapter that creates threads, submits turns, returns full text, and reports the correlated terminal state still implements whatever release rule the room writes. If that rule is “native success ⇒ release dependents,” prose blockers ship a review of incomplete work and fail Sequential review / Failures with no T3 bug.
- Two careful implementers can persist different graphs for “edit the prerequisite’s pending instruction.” Following revision 1 forever fails Sequential review (review of the old text). Silently retargeting to revision 2 can release against work the user no longer meant. Blocking without a specified UX fails Editing. The PRD does not pick.
- If cancel of a predecessor leaves dependents `queued` “waiting for named tasks,” those tasks never release and never show the Failures “reason visible” for cancel. If cancel is coded as fail, a user who cancelled a mistaken extra task looks like a broken predecessor. The diagram and the catalog sentence disagree.
- Steering false-success is the dual of prose-success: an assistant message that contains `@sol2 review this` or “I finished, start the review” must not create or complete a room task. Implementing mention-triggered dispatch, or treating commentary / idle / `thread.settled` as the assignment’s success, fails Manual coverage and Run correlation even with a perfect WebSocket.
- Context budget is independent of the 8,000-character MCP cap. Once the adapter can return full text, the room can still drop earlier sibling decisions, hide truncation, or invent a summarizer. That fails the Context row as product code. The budget number is unspecified, so “passed” is currently a judgment call.
- None of the above is solved by Jev, SemIf, or Herdr. Interpretation adapters do not write the scheduler. Herdr’s idle wait would make the false-success class worse, not better.

### Gaps
- No SQL DDL, JSON Schema, or transition appendix exists beside the PRD, so these failure modes are read from prose plus the mermaid diagram.
- This note does not claim the T3 adapter spike is done. `IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md` reports a Desktop-auth blocker on this machine; that is a T3-access precondition, not one of the product-shape holes above.
- No recorded local scheduler test exists. “Can still fail section 11” means the written oracle would fail if the unspecified rule is implemented the dangerous way, not that a failing test run is on disk.

## Does IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md claim “the plan is implementable as specified” overstate the product-shape note’s findings?

### Takeaway
Yes, as a headline. The assessment’s evidence supports a narrower claim: Phase 1 needs no model and, on the inspected T3 contracts, no fork for the adapter surfaces it checked. The product-shape review found that “as specified” is not complete enough to pass section 11 without inventing release, revision, cancel, retry, and context rules. Those findings are not absorbed by “every T3 capability exists.”

### Cited Findings

Every T3 capability the PRD depends on exists in the public contracts and in the locally installed build.” It then lists a Desktop credential precondition and three PRD inaccuracies (working directory name, PR citations, completion-event wording). “None of them change the product shape.” It did not re-verify Herdr. — [IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md](file:///Users/moonmoon/Projects/t3code-rooms/research/IMPLEMENTATION_ASSESSMENT.md)

- Product-shape note (2026-09-23 documentary review): “The durable-queue shape is specified enough to implement and unit-test the happy path.” “It is not specified enough to implement Phase 1 as accepted in section 11 without inventing commands, recovery transitions, and retry identity.” “The part that can sink Phase 1 after a basic adapter works is the identity of a run” — command-id reuse, which revision a dependent waits on, `dispatching` recovery, and prose-success release. ### Inferences
- The assessment overstates if “implementable as specified” is read as “an engineer can follow the PRD and pass section 11.” The product-shape note’s finding is that several section 11 oracles have no unique implementation in the spec, and the dangerous defaults fail those oracles after the adapter works.
- The assessment does not overstate the T3-surface claim it actually measured: create/attach/submit/observe/interrupt/permission-respond exist in contracts and in the local build, and interpretation is optional. That is compatible with “Phase 1 needs no model” and with “Herdr is not the path.”
- The phrase “None of them change the product shape” in the assessment refers to its three PRD corrections (directory name, PR numbers, completion events). It is easy to misread as a rebuttal of the product-shape note. It is not one: the assessment never lists mark-blocked, revision follow, cancel-to-blocked, or context-budget as gaps to close before coding.
- A report that wants both documents to be true should split the verdict: **T3 adapter implementable without a fork or a model, modulo Desktop auth; Phase 1 as a section 11 completion claim still blocked by unspecified product rules that an adapter cannot invent correctly by itself.**

### Gaps
- The two notes were written for different questions (T3 contracts vs product-shape completeness). There is no single author reconciliation in the repo.
- Whether implementers would in practice pick the safe revision/cancel defaults is not evidenced. Overstatement here is about the written claim versus the written holes, not about predicted shipping quality.
